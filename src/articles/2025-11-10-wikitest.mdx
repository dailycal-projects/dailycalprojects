In October of 2012, a Wikipedia user deleted a paragraph from the article on Mark Yudof, then President of the University of California.

<Wikidiff diff_id="yudof" />

This person, whoever they were, had taken it upon themselves to remove all references to Yudof’s opposition to increasing pensions and his own historically high pension. This person, (whoever they were), also happened to have made this edit from within the UC Office of the President.

Since its founding in 2001, Wikipedia has been shaped by UC students, staff, and faculty.

One of the unique qualities of Wikipedia that allowed it to grow so quickly and stay constantly updated is its lack of an account requirement. Anyone can visit a page and edit it without signing in or registering an account. Edits by non logged-in users are associated with their IP address as opposed to their account name. IP addresses are grouped into Autonomous Systems, large networks that are routed together. The University of California has separate Autonomous Systems for each of their campuses, as well as the Office of the President, and the National Laboratories it works with. Using custom software, we were able to search through Wikipedia’s expansive edit history to find any edits made by IP addresses within those Autonomous Systems.

It’s interesting to see what articles have actually been touched the most by different UC schools. There are lots of metrics to quantify this, like edit count, edit volume, and unique editors, so try clicking through all of them on your favorite UC campuses.
